    12. Using properties of the definite integral.

    \begin{equation*} \int_{1}^{21} f(x) \,dx - \int_{1}^{11} f(x) \,dx = \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \,dx \end{equation*}
    🔗
    where \(a=\) and \(b=\) .
    🔗
    Solution.
    First recall that
    \begin{equation*} \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \,dx + \int_{b}^{c} f(x) \,dx = \int_{a}^{c} f(x) \,dx \end{equation*}
    and therefore we can rearrange it to look like this:
    \begin{equation*} \int_{a}^{c} f(x) \,dx - \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \,dx = \int_{b}^{c} f(x) \,dx \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Applying that in this specific context, we find:
    \begin{equation*} \int_{1}^{21} f(x) \,dx - \int_{1}^{11} f(x) \,dx = \int_{11}^{21} f(x) \,dx \end{equation*}
    Thus we find \(a=11\) and \(b=21\text{.}\)
