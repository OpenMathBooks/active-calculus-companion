    5.

    Suppose that \(f\) is given for \(x\) in the interval \([0,12]\) by
    🔗
    \(x =\) 0 2 4 6 8 10 12
    \(f(x) =\) -17 -21 -22 -21 -18 -15 -11
    A. Estimate \(f'(2)\) using the values of \(f\) in the table.
    🔗
    \(f'(2)\approx\)
    🔗
    B. For what values of \(x\) does \(f'(x)\) appear to be positive?
    🔗
    (Give your answer as an interval or a list of intervals, e.g., (-infinity,8] or (1,5),(7,10) .)
    🔗
    C. For what values of \(x\) does \(f'(x)\) appear to be negative?
    🔗
    (Give your answer as an interval or a list of intervals, e.g., (-infinity,8] or (1,5),(7,10) .)
    🔗
    Solution.
    A. We could use the interval to the left or right of \(x = 2\) to estimate the derivative, or we could average the two. We do the last of these here. For the interval to the left, we have
    \begin{equation*} f'(2) \approx \frac{f(2) - f(0)}{2-0} = \frac{-21 + 17}{2} = -2. \end{equation*}
    For that to the right,
    \begin{equation*} f'(2) \approx \frac{f(4) - f(2)}{4-2} = \frac{-22 + 21}{2} = \frac{-1}2. \end{equation*}
    Our estimate is therefore
    \begin{equation*} f'(2) \approx \frac12\left(-2 + \frac{-1}2\right) = -1.25. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    B,C. We know that \(f'(x)\) is positive when \(f(x)\) is increasing and negative when \(f(x)\) is decreasing, so it appears that \(f'(x)\) is positive on the interval \(\left(4,12\right)\) and is negative on the interval \(\left(0,4\right)\text{.}\)
