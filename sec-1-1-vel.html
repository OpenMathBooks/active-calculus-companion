    Section 1.1 How do we measure velocity?

    You made it! This is the first section of the first chapter of the Active Calculus Companion. As with every section of this book, this section is divided into a subsection called Foundations which contains background material that you might be missing from previous courses, followed by a collection of exercises that can give you additional practice with the content of the corresponding section in Active Calculus (in the Calculus Practice subsection).
    🔗
    So: Foundations: What you should know and be able to do before you read the corresponding section in Active Calculus. Calculus Practice: What you should be able to do after you read the corresponding section in Active Calculus.
    🔗
    A lot of what is in the Foundations subsection will be familiar to you, and you can skip over what you already know. But we all have gaps in our mathematical understanding; that’s part of learning. So if you find yourself struggling with the activities or exercises in Active Calculus, checking here might help you fill in those gaps.
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.1 Foundations

    Section 1.1 of Active Calculus is about measuring velocity, first as an average velocity on an interval, and then moving toward the instantaneous velocity at a point (and what that even means).
    🔗
    In order to make sense of these ideas, we need to have some fluency with functions and function notation, as well as how we talk about points and intervals. The section also assumes you remember how to compute the slope of a line passing through two points.
    🔗

    Slopes of lines.

    Given any two points on the plane, there is exactly one line that passes through both points. The slope of that line is a measure of how steep the line is. The slope is computed as the change in the vertical direction divided by the change in the horizontal direction.

    Consider the function \(f(x) = -7 x - 6\) and find the following:
    🔗
    a) The average rate of change between the points \((-1, f(-1) )\) and \(( 3 , f( 3 ) )\) .
    🔗
    b) The average rate of change between the points \((a, f(a) )\) and \((b, f(b) )\) .
    🔗
    c) The average rate of change between the points \((x, f(x) )\) and \((x+h, f(x+h) )\) .
    🔗
    Solution.
    SOLUTION\((-1, f(-1))\)\(( 3 , f( 3 ))\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{ f( 3 ) - f(-1) }{3 - (-1)} = \frac{ -27 - 1 }{3 + 1} = \frac{ -28}{4 } = -7 \end{equation*}
    \((a, f(a))\)\(( b , f(b))\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{ f(b) - f(a) }{b - a} = \frac{ (-7 b - 6) - (-7 a - 6) }{b - a} = \frac{ -7 b + 7 a}{b-a} = \frac{ -7 (b-a) }{b-a} = -7 \end{equation*}
    \((x, f(x))\)\(( x+h , f(x+h))\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{ f(x+h) - f(x) }{(x+h) - x} = \frac{ (-7 (x+h) - 6) - (-7 x - 6) }{h} = \frac{ -7 x - 7 h -6 + 7 x + 6}{h} = \frac{ -7 h }{h} = -7 \end{equation*}
